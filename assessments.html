Practical entry point

Clarity Assessment and Clarity Audit are how most teams begin.

This is not a decision framework. It is a structured way to examine what an organization believes and whether reality still supports those beliefs.

That includes the triggers and thresholds used to monitor those beliefs. A trigger may fire correctly and still be poorly calibrated.

Clarity Assessment

A lighter review used to surface major claims, supporting evidence, key assumptions, and confidence levels around a project, plan, architecture, or method.

Clarity Audit

A deeper review used when commitments are already in motion and the cost of acting on stale beliefs is higher.

What an assessment examines

Claims What is the team asserting about reality, outcomes, or feasibility?
Evidence What currently supports those claims?
Assumptions What must remain true for the claims to hold?
Confidence Debt Where is confidence running ahead of what the current evidence can support?
Ownership Who is responsible for the consequences of being wrong?
Reassessment Trigger What signal, outcome, or threshold should force the claim back into review?
Trigger Claim What belief makes this signal and threshold meaningful in the first place?

Useful for live work

Project reviews, architecture reviews, startup evaluation, methodology reviews, postmortems, and reassessment audits.

Useful because it is structured

The goal is not rhetorical alignment. The goal is to make beliefs legible enough to examine and revise.

Useful because it can be repeated

Once the structure is visible, the same work can be revisited when new evidence appears.
