# The Clarity Decision Map

> **A decision is a commitment that constrains future options.**

The Clarity Decision Map helps organizations determine the correct **decision posture** based on the characteristics of the decision being made.

Decision posture determines:

- how much evidence is required
- how quickly a decision should be made
- how much coordination is needed
- how carefully risk must be managed

The Decision Map ensures that **decision effort matches decision consequence**.

---

# The Two Questions That Determine Decision Posture

Every decision can be evaluated using two simple questions:

1. **How bad is it if we are wrong?**
2. **How hard is it to undo?**

These two questions determine the appropriate **decision posture**.

---

# The Decision Map

```
                    IMPACT IF WRONG
                         HIGH
                           ↑
                           │
                           │
            Strategic      │      Guardrail
        deliberate         │   controlled risk
        commitment         │
                           │
                           │
         Experimental      │        Fast
         structured        │      move quickly
           learning        │       iterate
                           │
                           └────────────────────→
                          REVERSIBILITY
                        Easy                Hard
```

The map aligns **decision rigor** with the consequences of being wrong.

---

# Decision Postures

## Fast Decisions

Low impact and easily reversible.

Characteristics:

- minimal analysis
- rapid commitment
- quick iteration

Goal:

**Speed over certainty**

Examples:

- UI adjustments
- workflow improvements
- minor configuration changes
- small operational decisions

---

## Experimental Decisions

Used when uncertainty is high but impact is manageable.

Characteristics:

- controlled experimentation
- explicit learning objectives
- defined triggers for continuation or termination

Goal:

**Reduce uncertainty**

Examples:

- product experiments
- pilot programs
- A/B testing
- feature validation

---

## Guardrail Decisions

Operational decisions with meaningful risk.

Characteristics:

- defined constraints
- active monitoring
- predefined failure signals

Goal:

**Control operational risk**

Examples:

- infrastructure configuration
- security policies
- operational safety limits
- reliability thresholds

---

## Strategic Decisions

High impact and difficult to reverse.

Characteristics:

- deliberate evaluation
- strong evidence requirements
- cross-organizational coordination

Goal:

**Minimize irreversible mistakes**

Examples:

- entering a new market
- major architectural changes
- mergers and acquisitions
- foundational platform decisions

---

# Decision Rigor Gradient

Decision rigor should increase as:

- **impact increases**
- **reversibility decreases**

This creates a gradient of rigor across the map.

```
Fast → Experimental → Guardrail → Strategic
```

Fast decisions optimize for **speed**.

Strategic decisions optimize for **risk management and correctness**.

---

# Common Decision Failures

Organizations often fail because they **misclassify decisions**.

---

## Bureaucracy

A **Fast decision treated as Strategic**.

Symptoms:

- unnecessary approvals
- excessive analysis
- slow execution

Result:

The organization moves too slowly.

---

## Recklessness

A **Strategic decision treated as Fast**.

Symptoms:

- rushed commitment
- insufficient evidence
- ignored risks

Result:

Irreversible mistakes.

---

## Permanent Experimentation

An **Experimental decision that never collapses into commitment**.

Symptoms:

- endless pilots
- unclear decision triggers
- avoidance of commitment

Result:

Organizations learn without ever deciding.

---

# Teaching Shortcut

When evaluating a decision, ask two questions:

**How bad is it if we are wrong?**

**How hard is it to undo?**

These two questions determine the correct posture.

---

# Recommended Visual Design

For training materials and presentations, the Decision Map should be displayed as a **2×2 quadrant diagram**.

Recommended visual design:

- vertical axis labeled **Impact if Wrong**
- horizontal axis labeled **Reversibility**
- shading increases toward the **Strategic quadrant**
- each quadrant labeled with the posture name
- include example decisions in each quadrant

The visual should communicate this core principle:

> **Decision rigor increases as impact rises and reversibility decreases.**

---

# Why the Decision Map Matters

Many organizations apply the same decision process to every situation.

This produces:

- slow decisions where speed is needed
- reckless decisions where caution is required
- experiments that never lead to commitment

The Decision Map ensures organizations apply the **correct level of discipline to each decision**.

# Framework Principle

The purpose of the Decision Map is not to eliminate uncertainty.

Its purpose is to ensure organizations apply **the correct level of discipline to each decision**.

Clarity does not remove uncertainty.

Clarity makes decisions **safe despite uncertainty**.
