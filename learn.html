Learn by applying

Clarity is easier to understand when applied to a claim.

These exercises and worked examples are meant to be used, not just read. Each one starts with a claim and walks the reader back through evidence, assumptions, confidence, ownership, and reassessment.

Introduction

The core questions stay the same each time: What do we believe? Why do we believe it? What assumptions support it? How confident are we? What would change our minds? Who owns the consequences?

Exercises

Startup Exercise

Scenario

A founder believes customers want a new feature.

Claim

Customers want Feature X.

Questions

  • What evidence supports this claim?
  • Is this Discovery, Commitment, or Reassessment?
  • What assumptions are being treated as facts?
  • What would change our minds?
  • What is the smallest experiment that could reduce uncertainty?
Architecture Exercise

Scenario

A team believes microservices will improve scalability.

Claim

Microservices will improve scalability.

Questions

  • What problem is microservices expected to solve?
  • What evidence supports this?
  • What assumptions exist?
  • What happens if this claim is wrong?
  • What would trigger reassessment?
Insurance Exercise

Scenario

A family is deciding whether switching insurance plans will save money.

Claim

Switching plans will reduce cost without meaningful downside.

Questions

  • What visible costs are being compared?
  • What hidden costs might exist?
  • What assumptions support the claim?
  • What would cause reassessment?
  • Who bears the burden if the assumption is wrong?
Restaurant Exercise

Scenario

A high-end restaurant is short-staffed and a key ingredient delivery does not arrive.

Claim

Tonight’s planned menu can still be executed.

Questions

  • What changed?
  • Which decisions are correctable?
  • Which decisions are time-sensitive?
  • What assumptions must be reassessed immediately?
  • What is the best adaptive move?
Methodology Exercise

Scenario

A team proposes a new AI-assisted development methodology.

Claim

This methodology can be reused across different projects.

Questions

  • What evidence supports portability?
  • What project types have been tested?
  • What project types remain untested?
  • What would indicate the methodology does not generalize?
  • What should be validated next?

Worked Examples

Startup Worked Example

Scenario

A founder has heard repeated requests for a dashboard export feature and believes it is the missing step for closing more deals.

Claim

Customers want Feature X.

Posture

Discovery. The team has signals, but not enough support to treat demand as settled.

Evidence

Several prospects asked about exports during sales calls, and two current users said they would use it.

Assumptions

The requests represent broad demand, the feature is a buying factor rather than a nice-to-have, and the team understands what users mean by “export.”

Confidence

Moderate at best. There is signal, but it is still thin and probably biased toward the loudest users.

Reassessment Trigger

A small prototype or concierge test fails to change conversion, usage, or retention in the expected segment.

Clarity Observation

The founder may be treating interest as proof of demand. Clarity slows that jump and asks for the smallest test that separates curiosity from actual behavior.

Architecture Worked Example

Scenario

A platform team proposes splitting a growing application into microservices because deploy times are longer and some workloads spike at peak hours.

Claim

Microservices will improve scalability.

Posture

Reassessment. The system is already serving traffic, and the team is reconsidering whether the current architecture still fits demand.

Evidence

Peak load affects a few hot paths, deploys are slow, and some teams are stepping on each other operationally.

Assumptions

The main bottleneck is architectural coupling, the organization can absorb distributed-system complexity, and scaling pain cannot be solved more simply inside the current design.

Confidence

Mixed. The pain is real, but the link between that pain and microservices as the right answer is not yet proven.

Reassessment Trigger

If targeted improvements inside the monolith remove the hottest bottlenecks, or if service boundaries remain unclear after design review, the migration claim should be reconsidered.

Clarity Observation

The word “scalability” can hide several different problems. Clarity forces the team to state which problem they are actually trying to solve before committing to an expensive structural change.

How to Use These

Start with the claim

Do not begin with terminology. Begin by writing the belief in plain language.

Answer before debating

Use the questions to expose evidence, assumptions, and ownership before trying to persuade anyone.

Look for the next test

A good exercise usually ends with a better reassessment trigger, a smaller experiment, or a clearer owner.
