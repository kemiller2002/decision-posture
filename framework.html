Framework overview

Clarity is a framework for examining beliefs before they harden into action.

It treats decisions as only one part of a larger discipline: making claims explicit, testing the evidence behind them, naming what is assumed, and reassessing when reality changes.

Claim

A claim is a belief stated clearly enough to examine, challenge, support, or revise.

Posture

Clarity uses three postures: Discovery for exploration, Commitment for active reliance, and Reassessment for checking whether a prior commitment still fits reality.

Evidence

Evidence is what currently supports a claim. It may be direct observation, data, tests, history, or constrained experience.

Assumption

Assumptions are the unstated supports beneath a claim. Clarity tries to surface them before they become invisible sources of failure.

Confidence Debt

Confidence debt accumulates when an organization acts with more certainty than its evidence justifies.

Reassessment Trigger

A reassessment trigger is a condition or signal that should force a claim back into review.

Adaptation

Adaptation is the practical end state: beliefs change, commitments change, and behavior changes with them.

How the pieces relate

Claim → Evidence → Assumptions → Confidence → Ownership → Reassessment → Adaptation

Discovery

Use this posture when the organization is still learning what might be true. The emphasis is on exposing claims and assumptions, not defending them.

Commitment

Use this posture when a team is actively relying on a belief to guide execution, allocation, or policy.

Reassessment

Use this posture when time, evidence, or outcomes suggest a previously accepted belief may no longer hold.

Decision Posture still matters inside Clarity, but now as one concept within a broader system for managing beliefs, confidence, and revision.
